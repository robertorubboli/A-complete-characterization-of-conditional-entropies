\title{Lean Formalization of the Parameter Classification for Conditional Entropies}

\begin{abstract}
This note explains how Lean~4 verifies the necessary and sufficient parameter
conditions for the concrete conditional-entropy candidates studied in the
complete-proof paper, Ref.~\cite{rubboli2026parametersFull}. The formalization
covers all thirty-one numbered results: sixteen results in Sections~4 and~5 and
fifteen technical results in Appendix~A. For each result, the note identifies
one distinct Lean theorem with the same mathematical content, allowing the
reader to move directly from a statement in the paper to its formal proof. A
dependency diagram also shows how the main classification is built from the
underlying calculus, measure-theoretic, localization, and semiring/channel
results. Every supporting result is proved using Lean and Mathlib: the
development contains no unfinished proofs, project-defined axioms, or
additional unproved assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Lean correspondence}
\label{sec:lean-correspondence}

The formal development follows the complete analytic proofs in
Ref.~\cite{rubboli2026parametersFull}. It covers the endpoint-aware R\'enyi parameter,
real-power and entropy differentiation, signed integration, common
discretization, the two-block, three-block, and Shannon localizers, exact
tropical stationarity, all sufficiency and necessity branches, and the
original finite semiring and conditionally mixing channel lift. The source is
available at
\url{https://github.com/robertorubboli/A-complete-characterization-of-conditional-entropies}
on the repository's main branch.

A Lean file contains definitions and named mathematical statements together
with their proofs. Lean calls each named item a \emph{declaration}. A theorem
declaration records exactly which assumptions may be used and which conclusion
must be proved. When the project is built, Lean checks every step using
Mathlib, its standard mathematical library, and declarations that have already
been proved in the project. If a step is missing or does not follow, the build
fails.

Every numbered lemma, proposition, and remark in the complete-proof paper,
Ref.~\cite{rubboli2026parametersFull}, has one matching Lean theorem. The
theorem's name points directly to its formal proof. For example, Lemma~4.1 is
represented by \LeanDecl{ConditionalEntropy.fullDetailsLemma4_1}. A theorem
may use several smaller proved lemmas internally, just as a written proof may
refer to earlier results.

The formal proof ends with two final theorems. The theorem
\LeanDecl{mainClassification} states that, for the given probability measure
on the compactified R\'enyi parameter space, each concrete candidate is
monotone exactly when its parameter satisfies the stated admissibility
condition. Its only explicit input is the measure being classified; it needs
no additional hypothesis. The theorem
\LeanDecl{allConditionalEntropyAxioms} proves nonnegativity, zero-embedding
invariance, tensor additivity, fair-bit normalization, and embedded channel
monotonicity for every admissible candidate. All of its conditions are part of
the theorem itself, so it has no external input. Neither theorem uses a
project-defined axiom. In the complete-proof paper and in Lean, $\log$ is the
natural logarithm and an independent fair bit has value $\log 2$.

The two dependency diagrams show the proof at different levels of detail.
Figure~\ref{fig:lean-dependencies} is a short overview. Starting at either
green box and following the arrows downward shows which results are used in
that proof. White boxes represent numbered results from the complete-proof
paper, while blue boxes collect smaller supporting results proved in Lean.

The files \path{paper/dependency-graph.dot},

The sixteen results in Sections~4 and~5, including Remarks~5.4--5.5, have
sixteen different matching theorems. Their names begin with
\LeanDecl{ConditionalEntropy} and they are defined in
\path{ConditionalEntropy/FullDetailsStatements.lean}. Inside its proof, a
matching theorem may call smaller proved lemmas. The source therefore has more
names than the table, but the correspondence remains one-to-one: the table
lists exactly one complete theorem for each numbered manuscript result, and
the dependency graphs show the additional proof steps.

\subsection{What is checked}

The project uses Lean~4.32.2 and Mathlib~4.32.2. Its source contains no
\texttt{sorry} or \texttt{admit}, which are markers for unfinished proofs, and
no project-defined \texttt{axiom} or \texttt{opaque} declaration. Thus none of
the manuscript results has been inserted into the project as an unproved fact.
The final theorems require no extra project hypothesis. Like other Lean
developments based on Mathlib, the project uses Lean's standard logical
foundations, including classical choice, propositional extensionality, and
quotient soundness.

The command
\begin{verbatim}
powershell -File scripts/check.ps1
\end{verbatim}
performs the complete check. It rejects unfinished-proof markers, builds all
Lean files with warnings treated as errors, reports the standard logical
foundations used by the final theorems, and checks the 152 internal proof
records. It also reads all thirty-one result numbers and labels from the LaTeX
paper, verifies that their Lean names are distinct and appear in the same
order, and asks Lean to check every corresponding theorem. A successful run
therefore checks both the proofs and the paper-to-Lean correspondence. The
formalization covers the classification and the five entropy laws for all four
concrete candidate families, using the natural-log convention of the
complete-proof paper.
