\appendix
\section{Lean correspondence}
\label{app:lean-correspondence}

The accompanying Lean development follows the proof architecture of
Sections~4 and~5 of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23213}{the conditional-entropy manuscript}.
Figure~\ref{fig:lean-dependencies} gives the dependency graph, with arrows
pointing from a manuscript conclusion to its checked ingredients. Green boxes
are paper-facing coefficient cores and blue boxes are standalone bridge or
boundary proofs. Table~\ref{tab:lean-correspondence} records the
statement-by-statement correspondence.

The parenthetical value in each box is the number of Lean declarations
introduced with \decl{theorem} or \decl{lemma}. The counts are disjoint and
sum to 36. Every one of these declarations is checked with Lean~4.32.2 and
Mathlib~4.32.2. The source contains no \decl{sorry}, \decl{admit}, or
project-specific \decl{axiom}; a separate kernel audit prints the axiom
dependencies of the public declarations.

The word ``core'' is deliberate. The present development verifies the finite
coefficient and order-theoretic consequences of the manuscript. The
general-measure discretisation, differentiated R\'enyi integral, and
high-dimensional localisation limits remain explicit analytic boundaries.
They are neither asserted as Lean theorems nor hidden behind additional
axioms. The source is available at
\href{https://github.com/robertorubboli/conditional-entropy-characterisation}
{github.com/robertorubboli/conditional-entropy-characterisation}.

\subsection*{Content of the boxes and meaning of the arrows}

\paragraph{Counting convention.}
The green boxes contain the paper-facing conclusions proved at the finite
coefficient level. The blue boxes contain reusable ingredients. In particular,
the box labelled ``Scalar curvature witnesses'' is not a second copy of
Propositions~5.1--5.7. It contains five elementary real-variable lemmas used by
several of those propositions. The declarations assigned to different boxes
are disjoint, which is why the displayed numbers add to 36 without counting
any theorem twice.

\paragraph{Two kinds of implication.}
A solid arrow denotes an actual dependency between checked Lean declarations:
the theorem in the upper box invokes a theorem in the lower box, possibly
through an intermediate declaration listed in Table~\ref{tab:lean-correspondence}.
A dashed arrow has the meaning it has in the manuscript proof. It records the
route from a curvature calculation to monotonicity or to a finite semiring
counterexample, but that route presently crosses an analytic statement which
has not yet been formalized. Thus Figure~\ref{fig:lean-dependencies} is neither
merely an import graph nor a claim that the complete measure-theoretic
propositions have already been checked.

\paragraph{The sufficient direction.}
For a finitely supported parameter measure, the one-column function factorises
as a monomial of power means. The box ``Lemma 4.2 / Lemma B.2'' contains the
coefficient part of the corresponding curvature calculation. If
$\sum_i\beta_i=1$, the checked identity is
\[
  \left(\sum_i\beta_i a_i\right)^2-\sum_i\beta_i a_i^2
  =-\sum_i\beta_i\left(a_i-\sum_j\beta_j a_j\right)^2.
\]
Nonnegative coefficients therefore give the nonpositive curvature required
for the concave branch, while a negative coefficient supplies an explicit
direction of positive curvature. The two declarations in the ``Lemma 4.3
core'' box apply these sign facts to the positive-temperate coefficient family
and to the two-coefficient negative-temperate family. They formalize the
coefficient test after factorisation; the curvature of the power means, the
endpoint treatment, and the common discretisation used for a general measure
remain on the analytic side of the boundary.

The ``Appendix A implication'' box contains the finite algebraic passage from
one-column shape to conditioning monotonicity. Concavity together with positive
homogeneity gives superadditivity; convexity together with positive
homogeneity gives subadditivity. Summing these inequalities over a finite
row-stochastic mixing matrix proves monotonicity of the column lift in the
positive case and the reverse inequality used by the oppositely ordered
negative case. The dashed arrows from Lemma~4.3 and Proposition~4.8 indicate
that the manuscript combines this implication with the missing analytic shape
theorems. The single checked declaration attached to Proposition~4.8 records
only the resulting coefficient-level absence of an upper-support obstruction;
it does not yet prove concavity of the full R\'enyi barycentre.

\paragraph{The necessary direction.}
The five scalar witnesses are the algebraic endpoints of the perturbations in
Section~5. They express the following strict signs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a nonzero Shannon atom contributes $m^2>0$;
  \item a negative upper-tail coefficient gives $A^2-A>0$;
  \item a lower coefficient $B>1$ gives $B^2-B>0$;
  \item two positive coefficients admit an explicitly stationary direction
        with strictly negative quadratic curvature; and
  \item a negative upper-tail coefficient for a derivation gives $-A>0$.
\end{itemize}
These witnesses imply the green coefficient conclusions by solid arrows.
Proposition~5.1 combines the first three obstructions to eliminate a Shannon
atom, exclude support above~1, and bound the lower moment by~1.
Propositions~5.2--5.3 use the stationary two-positive-coefficient witness to
obtain the exceptional moment inequality. Proposition~5.6 uses the same
witness to rule out two separated positive upper cells. The negative tropical
box uses it with the quasi-convex second-order condition, and Proposition~5.7
uses the final sign to exclude positive measure above~1 for derivations.

\paragraph{Exact stationarity and finite counterexamples.}
The full high-dimensional argument differentiates at fixed finite dimension,
then integrates in the R\'enyi parameter, and only afterwards takes the
dimension limit. The two-block, three-block, and Shannon-point localisers turn
parts of the parameter measure into the coefficients appearing in the scalar
witnesses. Those localisation and dominated-convergence arguments are the
principal analytic boundary of the present development.

For the negative tropical branch, limiting stationarity is not enough.
Lemma~B.15 changes one block velocity by a quantity tending to zero so that the
first derivative vanishes exactly at the same finite dimension where
quasi-convexity is applied, while the limiting negative second derivative is
preserved. Appendix~B.6 then uses Taylor's theorem to turn a strict curvature
sign into two positive finite-dimensional endpoints: positive second
derivative violates concavity, negative second derivative violates convexity,
and negative second derivative at exact stationarity gives the midpoint
violation of quasi-convexity. The arrows to Lemma~B.15 and Appendix~B.6 are
dashed because the current coefficient theorems assume the resulting
curvature inequalities directly; they do not yet invoke the formalized
stationarity or midpoint declarations through a formalized localisation
theorem.
